# Employee Amplification

The Page's distribution layer. Without it, a Page post reaches roughly 1-2% of
followers and stops (`../../references/page-algorithm-heuristics.md`).

## The one thing that ruins every advocacy programme

**A plain reshare with nothing added is worse than not resharing.** It carries
no new information, barely circulates, and reduces the sharer's own future
distribution. 360Brew was built to discount low-credibility content signals,
and a dozen identical reshares of one corporate post is the cleanest such
signal available.

This kills the standard playbook — "here's the post, everyone please share" —
which produces a dozen clones that each reach almost nobody and quietly damage
the reach of the twelve people who helped. The programme feels like
participation and measures like nothing.

So **every kit this skill produces gives each person their own angle and their
own words.** Never one caption to paste. The Page post is raw material; each
employee post is a different post about the same thing. Full mechanics and the
supporting figures: `../../references/amplification-mechanics.md`.

## When to use

- "How do we get employees posting?" / "employee advocacy"
- "Nobody sees our Page posts" / "how do we get more reach"
- A specific Page post needs its amplification kit
- "Should we boost this post?" → the answer usually involves a Thought Leader
  Ad on a person's post, not a Page boost
- Standing up a programme from scratch

## Mode: per-post kit

The everyday job. For one Page post:

### 1. Pick the people

- **Relevance beats rank.** The engineer who built the thing is a better
  amplifier than the CEO who did not. Consistency beats title: an engaged
  mid-level person who posts regularly is worth more than an executive who
  needs chasing every time.
- **Match the post to the person's real stake.** A proof post goes to whoever
  delivered the work; a hiring post to the team who will interview; a data drop
  to whoever ran the numbers.
- **Three to six people.** Beyond that the angles repeat, which is the clone
  problem again.
- **Check the ceiling.** One company-adjacent post per person per week.
  Somebody resharing twice a week turns their feed into a company channel and
  loses the audience that made them useful.

### 2. Assign a rung each

From `../../references/amplification-mechanics.md`:

| Rung | Action | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | React | First hour only, to help the post clear its initial distribution test |
| 2 | **Substantive comment** | Underrated. Adds information the post lacked, feeds the comment-depth signal the ranker weighs most, costs two minutes |
| 3 | Reshare with a personal take | Minimum ~40 words of their own framing before the reshare |
| 4 | **An original post on the same subject** | Highest value by a distance. No link to the Page post at all |

Rung 4 is the goal. Two posts about one thing from two accounts beat one post
shared twice.

### 3. Write each person's draft in their voice, not the Page's

This is the work. For each person produce:

- **Their angle in one line** — what they specifically know about this that the
  Page post did not say.
- **A draft** at their assigned rung, first person, in their register. If you
  have seen their writing, match it. If not, keep it plain and tell them to
  change the wording — a stiff draft they edit is better than a polished one
  they paste.
- **What to do if someone asks a follow-up** in their comments, in one line.

Two rules: **no two drafts may share an opening line**, and each must contain
something the Page post did not. If you cannot find a distinct angle for the
fifth person, use four.

### 4. Allocate the notify budget

**Once every seven days per Page.** Mark whether this is the post worth it.
Spending it on a routine update teaches employees the notification is noise,
and the lever stops working.

### 5. Deliver the kit

```
## Amplification kit — [post], [date]

Page post: [link or title] · Published: [date/time]
Notify employees: [yes — this is the week's slot / no — reason]

### [Name] — rung [n]
Angle: [one line]
Draft:
  [their text]
If asked [likely question]: [one line]

[repeat per person]

### First hour
[who reacts, who comments first]

### Not asked this week
[who is being rested, and why]
```

That last section matters: it shows the programme is protecting people rather
than draining them, which is what sustains participation.

## Mode: programme launch

A 14-day sequence to stand one up. Detail and governance:
`references/launch-and-governance.md`.

**Days 1-3 — Find the willing.** Ask, do not announce. Three to five people who
already post, or want to. A programme launched by all-hands email starts with
compliance and ends with silence.

**Days 4-6 — Remove the blank page.** For each person, three angles from their
own work that they could post about. Most people do not decline advocacy; they
decline the blank page.

**Days 7-10 — Ship rung 2 and 3.** Substantive comments and takes on one Page
post. Low stakes, immediate visible result.

**Days 11-14 — One rung 4 each.** An original post. Then show each person their
own numbers against the Page's for the same subject, which is the moment the
programme either earns belief or does not.

**Then:** a weekly rhythm — one Page post with a kit, one notify slot, a
monthly reading of the Employee Advocacy analytics tab.

## Mode: Thought Leader Ad selection

When the user asks about boosting. The honest framing is that TLAs sponsor an
**individual's** organic post, which is how a brand buys around the Page reach
problem.

Constraints that break plans if discovered late:

- The **member must approve** the sponsorship.
- **Only posts from the last 30 days** are eligible, so the organic pipeline
  has to run continuously.
- **Eligible:** text, single image, video, article and newsletter posts.
  **Not eligible:** document posts, polls, multi-image posts. This bites,
  because document posts are the best organic format and the worst TLA
  candidate.
- Objectives are limited to awareness, engagement and video views — LinkedIn
  lists no lead-gen or website-visits objective.

**And read the benchmarks carefully.** LinkedIn reports around 252% higher CTR
and 62% lower CPC against single-image ads, and independent data puts median
TLA CTR near 2.68% against about 0.42% for single-image. But the same dataset
puts click-through *to landing page* at roughly 0.29-0.43%, and a paired
within-account comparison found landing-page CTR effectively identical between
TLAs and Page sponsored content.

The resolution: TLA "clicks" include post expansions and profile views. TLAs
win on **attention and cost per engagement**; the evidence that they win on
**traffic** is weak. Plan them as a credibility instrument, never promise a
pipeline figure from a CTR benchmark, and separate in-feed from landing-page
clicks in any report.

## Hard rules

- **Never produce one caption for everyone.** The clone signal is the whole
  failure mode.
- **Never make participation mandatory**, and never recommend a leaderboard.
  Compliance-driven programmes produce robotic posts, and robotic posts are the
  ones that get discounted.
- **One notify per seven days.** A hard platform limit.
- **Protect the volunteers.** One company-adjacent post per person per week.
- **Name consent.** Nobody is drafted for publicly without their agreement
  (`../../references/proof-library.md` §7).
- **Never write a claim into an employee's draft** that is not cleared in the
  proof library. Their account, their credibility, same evidence bar.
- **Never impersonate.** These are drafts for a person to edit and publish, and
  the kit should say so explicitly.

## Anti-patterns

- A Slack message saying "please share" with the post link.
- A leaderboard, or tying advocacy to performance review.
- Asking everyone. Three people with a stake beat thirty without one.
- Rung 1 only. A programme of reactions is a programme that has not started.
- Spending the notify slot weekly on whatever happened to be posted.
- Measuring posts-shared rather than reach-of-employee-posts-versus-Page.
- Promising a lead number from a TLA CTR benchmark.
