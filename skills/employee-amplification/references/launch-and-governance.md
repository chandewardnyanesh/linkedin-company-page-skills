# Launch sequence and governance

## The 14-day launch, in detail

### Days 1-3 — Find the willing

**Do not announce the programme.** An all-hands email about "our new employee
advocacy initiative" starts the thing as a compliance obligation, and
compliance produces exactly the robotic posts the ranker discounts.

Instead, identify three to five people individually:

- Who already posts on LinkedIn, even occasionally
- Who has said something interesting about the work in a meeting
- Who is hiring, and therefore has a reason of their own
- The founder, if they will actually do it rather than delegate it

Ask each one privately, and ask for something small: *"would you be willing to
add a comment to one post a week with the thing you know that we left out?"*
That is rung 2, it takes two minutes, and it is a real ask rather than a
programme.

Record who said no. They are not asked again this quarter.

### Days 4-6 — Remove the blank page

Most people do not decline advocacy. They decline the blank page.

For each participant, write down **three angles from their own work** they
could post about. Specific ones: "the reconciliation step clients always want
to skip", not "your expertise in data". Show them the list. The common response
is to reject all three and offer a fourth, which is the best possible outcome —
they now have a topic they chose.

### Days 7-10 — Ship rung 2 and rung 3

Pick one Page post. Produce the kit (per-post mode in the parent skill). Each
participant does a substantive comment or a reshare with a take.

Low stakes, visible same-day result, and it establishes the pattern that
amplification means *adding something*.

### Days 11-14 — One rung 4 each

An original post, in their own voice, no link to the Page.

Then show each person **their own numbers against the Page's** for the same
subject. This is the moment the programme earns belief or does not, and no
amount of internal advocacy substitutes for someone seeing that their post
reached four times what the Page post did.

### From day 15 — the rhythm

- One Page post a week with a kit
- One notify slot spent deliberately
- A monthly read of the Employee Advocacy analytics tab
- A quarterly check on who is tired

## Governance that is not a mandate

The governance question is usually asked as "how do we make sure they post the
right thing", which is the wrong question. People post less when supervised and
the supervised posts perform worse.

The useful frame: **what does someone need to know so they can post without
asking?**

### A one-page brief, not an approval process

Give every participant:

- **What they can always say** — the cleared claims from
  `../../../references/proof-library.md`, in plain language, as a short list.
- **What needs a check first** — customer names, performance figures,
  anything about an incident, pricing.
- **What they must never say** — the §10 limits from
  `../../../references/company-profile.md`, and confidential material.
- **Who to ask**, and the realistic turnaround.
- **What happens if they get it wrong** — and the honest answer should be "we
  fix it and nobody is in trouble", because any other answer ends
  participation.

One page. An approval queue for employee posts defeats the purpose: the value
is that the post comes from a person, and a person's post that went through
marketing review reads like marketing.

### Where approval genuinely is required

Narrow list, and say why:

- A claim not on the cleared list
- Anything about an incident, outage or complaint
- A named customer
- Anything in a regulated category
  (`../../../references/claims-and-compliance.md` §4)

Everything else goes out without review.

### Things that destroy programmes

- **Leaderboards.** They convert a voluntary act into a measured one, and the
  people who post least feel supervised.
- **Tying it to performance review.** Guarantees participation and guarantees
  the posts are worthless.
- **A content library of pre-approved captions.** This is the clone problem
  with a budget.
- **Asking the same three people every week.** They burn out in two months and
  their audience leaves first.
- **Correcting someone's voice.** If a participant's post is clunky but
  accurate, leave it. It outperforms a polished corporate version, and editing
  someone's voice is how you lose them.

## What to measure

From the Page's Employee Advocacy tab plus the posts themselves. Definitions:
`../../../references/metrics-taxonomy.md`.

| Metric | Why | Target shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Participation rate** (posted ÷ asked) | The health metric | Stable or rising; falling means burnout or a mandate creeping in |
| **Employee post reach vs Page post reach**, same subject | The number that justifies the programme internally | Employee side several times higher |
| **Rung distribution** | Whether the programme has actually started | Movement toward rungs 3-4 over a quarter |
| **Substantive comments**, hand-counted | Quality, and the signal the ranker weighs most | Rising |
| **Participants resting** | Whether volunteers are protected | Some, always |

Deliberately not measured: posts shared (rewards rung 1), total impressions
(rewards volume), and anything that ranks individuals against each other.

## The conversation when someone says no

Expect it, and accept it immediately. The useful follow-up is not persuasion:

> "That's fine. If it changes, the ask is one comment a week, not a posting
> habit."

Someone who declined and later volunteers is a better participant than someone
who was pushed. And a programme visibly tolerating refusal is a programme the
undecided are willing to join.
