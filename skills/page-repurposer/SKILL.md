# Page Repurposer

One source, several assets, across surfaces. The highest-leverage work in the
bundle, because the research is already paid for.

## Why atomisation rather than reformatting

The usual version of "repurpose the blog" produces a post that summarises the
blog and links to it, which performs badly for two separate reasons: external
links in the body are deprioritised, and a summary gives a reader no reason to
click.

Atomisation is different. One substantial source contains several independent
assets — a finding, a process, a counter-intuitive detail, a customer's
objection — and each of those is a native asset in its own right. A 2,000-word
report is not one post. It is a carousel, three posts, a newsletter issue and a
poll, spread over a month.

The second principle: **a repurposed asset has to stand alone.** If it only
makes sense as a trailer for the source, it is an advert for a link.

## When to use

- "Repurpose this" / "reuse this content" / "turn this into LinkedIn posts"
- A blog, report, case study, webinar, podcast, deck, whitepaper or email
- "Make this into a carousel"
- Moving a founder's personal post onto the Page, or the reverse
- After `authority-article-writer` produces an article that needs distribution

## Steps

### 1. Read the source properly

Fetch or read it in full. Then extract, before deciding any format:

- **Every claim with a number**, and where the source says it came from
- **Every process or sequence** — the raw material for a carousel
- **Every counter-intuitive detail** — the best post hooks live here
- **Every objection or question** the source answers
- **Every named person, customer or artefact**

If the source is long, read it in sections rather than skimming. Repurposing
from a skim produces assets built on the source's headings, which is how you
get four posts that all say the same thing.

### 2. Clear the claims — including from your own material

**Every figure carried across needs a `../../references/proof-library.md`
entry, even when the source is the company's own blog.** This is the step most
often skipped and the reason stale claims get republished with fresh
timestamps: a number on the company's own site is a sentence someone wrote,
possibly in 2019, not evidence
(`../../references/untrusted-content.md` rule 3).

For a third-party source: it is cited with source and date, never restated as
the company's own finding. And check the source is quotable at all — a
client's report or a paywalled study usually is not.

### 3. Map source to surfaces

| Source | Produces |
|---|---|
| **Long blog / whitepaper / report** | 1 document carousel (the spine) + 2-3 posts (one per finding) + 1 newsletter issue + 1 poll |
| **Case study** | 1 P1 proof post + 1 carousel (the process) + 1 P11 post from the objection the client raised |
| **Webinar / video** | 1 carousel (the structure) + 2-3 posts (the best answers from Q&A) + native clips. Never a link to the recording as a post |
| **Podcast episode** | 1 post per substantive question + 1 carousel of the framework discussed |
| **Deck** | 1 carousel — closest fit, but rebuild it. A deck exported to PDF is not a carousel |
| **Founder's personal post** | 1 Page version, reframed — see below |
| **Email or proposal** | 1 P11 post (the question it answered) + 1 P7 post (the process it described) |
| **Internal doc / runbook** | 1 P7 process reveal + 1 A7 process manual, subject to what may be disclosed |

### 4. Reframe for the surface, do not transplant

Each surface has a different contract, and copying across them is what makes
repurposed content feel second-hand:

- **Blog → post.** A blog opens with context; a post cannot. The post's hook is
  usually the blog's most specific sentence, which is often in the middle.
- **Anything → carousel.** 5-12 slides, slide 1 carries the whole hook, one
  idea per slide, link in the caption because a link inside the PDF is not
  tappable. Deliver a slide-by-slide outline plus the caption
  (`../../references/page-surfaces.md` §Document post specs).
- **Personal post → Page post.** The hard one. A personal post's power is
  usually first-person stakes, and a Page has none. The move is to convert the
  "I" into a named person inside the Page's voice, or to replace the personal
  stake with an aggregate: one person's experience becomes the pattern across
  forty clients (`../../references/brand-voice-rules.md` §The attribution rule).
  If the post's whole value was the individual's vulnerability, **say it should
  stay on the personal profile.** Some posts do not have a Page version.
- **Page post → personal post.** Easier and more valuable, given the reach
  gap. Strip the corporate framing, give it back to whoever actually did the
  work, and let them write in first person. This is `employee-amplification`
  rung 4 and it is the best outcome of a repurposing session.
- **Video → native clips.** Upload natively. A YouTube link is an external link
  and is deprioritised.

### 5. Sequence it

Do not publish the set at once. A month-shaped sequence
(`page-content-calendar`):

- Carousel and article 3-7 days apart, so the asset gets two distribution
  events rather than one
- Findings posts spread across following weeks
- Poll last, as the follow-up that generates its own post

### 6. Voice pass, because repurposed copy arrives in the source's voice

A blog's voice is not a LinkedIn voice, a deck's voice is bullet points, and a
transcript's voice is speech. Hand every output to `page-humanizer`, and expect
T2 structural work rather than T1 surface edits.

### 7. Approval

One card per asset, or one card for the plan plus cards per asset as they are
drafted. Each carries its claims and their sources. For the plan, show the
source, the assets, the sequence, and what you deliberately did not use.

## Hard rules

- **Every carried-over figure gets re-cleared**, including from the company's
  own material.
- **Third-party sources are cited with source and date**, never absorbed as the
  company's own finding.
- **Check the source is quotable.** A client's deck, an internal document or a
  paywalled report may not be, and this check belongs before drafting, not
  after.
- **Each asset stands alone.** If it only works as a trailer for the source, it
  is not an asset.
- **Never publish the set simultaneously.** Four assets in a day is one day of
  content and three wasted.
- **A deck exported to PDF is not a carousel.** Slide density, aspect ratio and
  text size are all wrong. Rebuild it.
- **Some posts have no Page version.** Say so rather than producing a hollow
  corporate rewrite of someone's personal story.

## Anti-patterns

- A post that summarises the blog and links to it.
- Four posts that are the blog's four headings.
- A 40-slide carousel from a 40-page report. Pick the spine.
- Posting the webinar recording link as the content.
- Copying the blog's opening paragraph as the post hook. A blog opens with
  context; context is not a hook.
- Repurposing something that did not work in the first place. Check how the
  source performed before spending a month on it.
- Running the same source through again three months later and presenting it as
  new.
