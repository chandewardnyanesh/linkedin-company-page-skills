# Pillar sets and cadence models

Starting points, to be adapted from `../../../references/company-profile.md` §7
rather than used as-is.

## Pillar sets by business type

A pillar needs **evidence behind it**, not interest. If
`../../../references/proof-library.md` holds nothing under a pillar, it is an
aspiration and the slots assigned to it will be filled with adjectives.

### B2B services / consulting

| Pillar | What it is | Evidence it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **The work, shown** | How engagements actually run, what they catch | A real process (P7, A7) |
| **Proof** | Results, with measured baselines | Cleared proof rows (P1) |
| **The category argument** | The position on how the problem should be framed | A defensible opinion, held publicly (P6, A3) |
| **Buyer education** | How to evaluate the category, including where you lose | Honesty about trade-offs (P13, A6) |

### Product / SaaS

| Pillar | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Build in public** | What shipped, what broke, what was cut (P2, P12) |
| **Usage data** | What the product reveals about the category — the moat (P4, A2) |
| **The job, not the feature** | The workflow the product sits inside (P7, P11) |
| **Customer outcomes** | Proof, with mechanisms (P1) |

### Local / regional services (real estate, trades, professional services)

Different economics entirely: the audience is smaller, geographically bound,
and often not active on LinkedIn at all. Check
`../../../references/icp-firmographics.md` §5 before planning anything — for some
of these sectors the honest answer is that the Page's only job is to be the
credibility check a buyer runs after meeting you elsewhere, and a four-post
week is wasted effort.

| Pillar | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Market reality** | What is actually happening locally, with numbers (P4) |
| **The process, demystified** | What a buyer does not know they need to know (P7, A1) |
| **Transactions** | Completed work, within compliance limits (P1) |
| **The honest no** | What you talk clients out of (P5) |

Regulated sectors: check `../../../references/claims-and-compliance.md` §4 before
a single slot is planned. In Indian real estate, for instance, RERA
registration disclosure and constraints on possession, approval and amenity
claims apply to advertising, and a content calendar that ignores that is a
calendar of compliance incidents.

### Agency / studio

| Pillar | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Work, with the thinking** | The brief, the constraint, the decision (P2) |
| **Teardowns** | Artefacts and practices, never named competitors (P3, A4) |
| **How we work** | Process, pricing posture, what we refuse (P5, P7) |
| **The people** | Named, with what they actually did (P16) |

## Cadence models

Pick by the hours available, and set it to the **worst month**. A model held
for a year beats a better model abandoned in week six.

### Minimum viable — ~2 hours/week

- 1 Page post/week — usually P11, the customer question, because it needs no
  new research
- 1 heavy asset/quarter → carousel + article
- Newsletter: monthly, or not yet
- Amplification: 2-3 people, rung 2 (substantive comments)

Honest and sustainable for a solo operator. The Page stays alive, the static
fields do the credibility work, and the quarterly asset gives employees
something to carry.

### Standard — ~5 hours/week

- 2-3 Page posts/week
- 1 heavy asset/month → carousel + article + 2 pull-out posts
- Newsletter: monthly
- Amplification: 4-6 people, mixed rungs, notify budget spent weekly

The default this bundle plans against.

### Committed — ~12 hours/week, or a dedicated person

- 3-4 Page posts/week
- 1 heavy asset/month plus 1 secondary
- Newsletter: fortnightly
- Amplification: a running programme (`skills/employee-amplification/`)
- Paid layer: Thought Leader Ads on the founder's organic posts

### What none of these do

**Daily posting.** It is not a step above "committed"; it is a different and
worse strategy for a Page. It trains followers to scroll past, it exhausts the
evidence supply within weeks, and the resulting filler is what the Page gets
judged on. The exception is a live campaign window where tempo is carrying a
launch, and that is a fortnight, not a posture.

## Sequencing a month

```
Week 1   Position-taking post (P5/P6/P13)        → comments
         Customer question (P11)                 → inbound
Week 2   Heavy asset: article or newsletter      → the month's anchor
         Proof post (P1)                         → trust
Week 3   Document carousel from the asset        → saves, reshares
         Customer question (P11)
Week 4   Pull-out finding from the asset         → reach via employees
         Post-mortem or honest no (P12/P5)       → trust
```

Why this shape: the position-taking post early generates the comments that tell
you what the month's audience cares about; the heavy asset lands mid-month with
room for follow-up; the carousel follows the article by 3-7 days rather than
accompanying it, so the asset gets two distribution events instead of one.

## The capacity conversation

When a user asks for more than §9 supports, put the trade-off in writing rather
than quietly padding:

> "Four posts a week is two heavy assets a month at your current evidence
> supply, or it is two good posts and two fillers. The fillers cost more
> follower trust than the quiet weeks would. Which do you want?"

Then plan whichever they choose, and record the choice in the plan's
"What this plan deliberately does not do" section.
