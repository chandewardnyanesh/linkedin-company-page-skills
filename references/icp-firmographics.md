# ICP and firmographics

The definition used to score followers, engagers, trends and leads. Without it,
every "is this working?" question collapses into a follower count, and a Page
can grow followers monthly while reaching fewer buyers than it did in January.

Used by `page-performance-report` (follower composition), `trend-radar`
(whether a trend matters to people who buy), `page-engagement-desk` (which
commenter is worth a reply) and `page-content-calendar` (who each week is for).

> Filled, this is commercially sensitive. Same handling as the other templates.

**Title variants matter mechanically.** LinkedIn follower demographics and
engager lists give you job titles as typed, so the scoring needs the real
variants — "Head of Ops", "Operations Manager", "GM Operations", "Plant Head"
are one role with four labels. Write them all down.

## 3. Triggers

What makes an account start looking. These are what `trend-radar` watches for,
and a trend that touches no trigger is interesting rather than useful.

- Events inside the company: (funding, new system, a hire, an audit, growth
  past a threshold, a failure)
- Events outside it: (regulation, a deadline, a cost shift, a competitor move)
- Seasonal: (financial year end, budget cycle, festive or holiday slowdowns)

## 4. Scoring

A follower or engager is scored into one of four tiers. Keep it crude — a
precise model built on job titles is false precision.

| Tier | Definition | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Buyer** | Right role, right account type | Worth a named follow-up and a real reply |
| **Influencer** | Wrong role, right account | Nurture; they forward things internally |
| **Peer** | Competitor, agency, consultant | Fine to have, never counted as reach |
| **Noise** | Students, job seekers, unrelated sectors, bots | Excluded from every reported number |

Target composition — set this, and report against it rather than against
follower growth:

- Buyer: ___% · Influencer: ___% · Peer: ___% · Noise: ___%

**The honest reading of a peer-heavy audience:** a Page whose followers are 60%
peers is producing content that impresses the industry rather than the market.
That is a content problem, not an audience problem, and the fix is in
`page-content-calendar` and `page-formats.md` — write to the buyer's question,
not to the peer's standard.
