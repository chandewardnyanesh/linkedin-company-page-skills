# Competitor set

Who else is talking to this audience, and what they have left unsaid.

Used by `competitor-page-analyzer` for the content comparison, by
`page-optimizer` for positioning language, and by `page-content-calendar` to
aim a month at white space rather than at whatever everyone else posted.

## 1. The set

Keep it to five or six. A list of twenty is a list nobody maintains.

| Company | LinkedIn Page | Website | Type | Why they are in the set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |

Types, because they are watched for different reasons:

- **Direct** — a buyer would shortlist you both.
- **Category-adjacent** — solves the same problem a different way.
- **Status quo** — the spreadsheet, the internal hire, the "do nothing". Often
  the real competitor and the one that never appears on a competitor list.
- **Attention competitor** — not a commercial rival, but owns the conversation
  your buyers read. Frequently a consultancy or an individual creator rather
  than a company, and frequently the most informative Page in the set.

## 2. What to record per competitor

Updated when `competitor-page-analyzer` runs. Observation only — no inference
about their strategy, because their posting is weak evidence of their intent.

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Followers, and date checked | Composition matters more than count if visible |
| Posting cadence | Posts per week over the last 8 weeks |
| Format mix | How much is document / text / video / poll / article |
| Newsletter | Name and subscriber count if public. A competitor with a newsletter has owned distribution you do not |
| Pillars | The 3-4 subjects they actually post about |
| Their strongest post of the period | And what it was doing (map to `page-formats.md`) |
| What they never post about | **The most valuable row in the table** |
| Employees posting about work | Count. This is their real reach, not the Page |
| Tagline and About positioning | Verbatim — it shows which words they are claiming |

## 3. LinkedIn's own Competitor analytics

Page admin view → Analytics → Competitors, desktop only. Nominate the Pages
above.

**One caveat that invalidates most competitive reports:** LinkedIn calculates
competitor engagement differently from your own, so the columns are not
directly comparable as shown. The workaround is to **add your own Page to the
competitor list** and read your row inside that view, which puts both numbers
on the same basis. Do this before quoting any comparison.

## 4. White space

The output that justifies the exercise. Fill this after an analysis run.

| Unclaimed subject | Evidence nobody is covering it | Do we have standing to own it? | Evidence we hold |
|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |

Standing is the gate. A subject nobody covers because nobody in the category
can speak to it credibly is not white space — it is a subject you would also be
guessing about. Check `proof-library.md` before claiming a gap.

## 6. What not to do with this file

- **Never name a competitor in a post.** Default position in
  `claims-and-compliance.md` §3. This file informs what you say, never who you
  say it about.
- **Never copy their calendar.** Matching a competitor's cadence with weaker
  evidence produces worse content at the same cost.
- **Do not infer strategy from posting.** A quiet Page may be the one winning
  the market. Posting volume is evidence about marketing capacity, nothing else.
