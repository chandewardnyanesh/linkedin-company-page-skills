# Page post formats

Sixteen structures that work when the author is a company rather than a person.

## Why personal hook formulas do not transfer

The personal-brand formula libraries are built on devices a Page cannot use
honestly. A confession needs someone to confess. A vulnerability hook needs a
self. "I was broke at 24" has no company equivalent, and the attempt produces
the worst genre on the platform: a corporate entity performing feelings.

What a Page has instead is **access a person does not have**: the whole book of
business, the aggregate numbers, the thing that broke in production, the
pattern across forty clients, the internal process, the real cost of the work.
A person can only report their own experience. A Page can report a population.
Every format below is a way of spending that advantage.

So the substitution is: where a personal post would use emotional stakes, a
Page post uses **evidence and specificity**. That is also the only thing that
survives the credibility filters described in `page-algorithm-heuristics.md`.

## Pick by job, not by taste

Start from what the post is for. A Page post that is not doing one of these
jobs should not be written, however good the hook is.

| Job | Reach for |
|---|---|
| Make a buyer believe you can do the work | P1, P4, P7, P13 |
| Make the Page worth following | P3, P4, P6, P12 |
| Earn substantive comments (the 2026 ranker's strongest signal) | P5, P6, P11, P13, P15 |
| Earn saves and reshares (which is how a Page borrows reach) | P4, P7, P8, P13 |
| Attract talent | P9, P16 |
| Fill an event or launch | P14, P10 |
| Give employees something they will actually reshare | P1, P2, P4, P8, P16 |

That last row matters more than it looks. Given the reach economics, **a Page
post's real audience is often the company's own employees**, who decide whether
to carry it to a network that is 5-20x larger. Write for the employee who has
to decide whether resharing this makes them look sharp or makes them look like
a billboard. See `amplification-mechanics.md`.

## The formats

Each carries a skeleton, a compressed example, and the way it fails. Fill the
bracketed slots from `company-profile.md` and `proof-library.md` — never invent
a figure to complete a skeleton. If the slot cannot be filled truthfully, the
format is the wrong one for this week.

---

### P1 — Proof Ledger
**For:** making a buyer believe. **Goal:** trust, employee reshares.

```
[Specific before-state number] → [specific after-state number], [timeframe].
[One sentence on who the client was, by type if not by name.]
[What was actually changed — the mechanism, not the benefit.]
[The part that was harder than expected.]
[What it cost them / how long it took.]
[A question about the reader's version of the same problem.]
```

> "Invoice approval went from 40 minutes to 6 at a 180-person auto components
> maker. The fix was not AI. It was that three systems disagreed about what a
> part number looked like, and we made them agree. The reconciliation took
> eleven days, most of it reading other people's spreadsheets."

**Fails when** the numbers are rounded to marketing ("improved efficiency by
50%"), or the mechanism is withheld to protect a sales conversation. A
withheld mechanism reads as a case study with the interesting part removed,
because that is what it is.

---

### P2 — Build Log
**For:** proving the company is made of competent people. **Goal:** trust, talent.

```
[Named person] spent [specific time] on [specific problem].
[What they tried first, and why it did not work.]
[What worked.]
[What is still unsolved.]
```

**Fails when** nobody is named, or the problem is too clean. A build log with no
dead end in it is a brochure.

---

### P3 — Teardown
**For:** demonstrating judgement. **Goal:** followers, comments.

```
[A specific artefact in the category — a pricing page, an RFP, a spec, a
widely-shared claim.]
[Three things it gets right.]
[The one thing that will cause a problem, and when that problem shows up.]
[What you would do instead.]
```

**Fails when** the target is a named competitor. Tear down an artefact or an
industry practice, never a company. Naming a competitor converts a credibility
post into a liability post and `claims-and-compliance.md` blocks it.

---

### P4 — Original Data Drop
**For:** the single best thing a Page can publish. **Goal:** saves, reshares, authority.

```
We looked at [n] [units] of our own [data source] from [date range].
[The headline finding, as a number.]
[Two supporting findings.]
[The finding that surprised you / contradicted what you expected.]
[Method, in one sentence, including the sample's limits.]
```

> "Across 46 discovery calls this year, 31 of the companies asking us for an
> AI roadmap could not name which system held their customer master. Not
> wouldn't. Couldn't."

**Why it is the strongest format:** nobody else can publish it. It cannot be
competed away, it is the natural seed for an article (`article-architectures.md`
A2), and it is the one thing employees reshare without being asked. A Page that
produces four of these a year has a moat.

**Fails when** the sample is too small to carry the claim and the post hides
that. State n. State the limits. An honest small sample is more credible than a
vague large one.

---

### P5 — The Honest No
**For:** credibility through cost. **Goal:** comments, inbound quality.

```
We turned down [type of work] this [month/quarter].
[Why — the real reason, which should cost you something to say.]
[What we told them to do instead.]
[Who we are the right answer for.]
```

**Fails when** the "no" is fake humility that flatters the company ("we only
work with clients who are serious about growth"). The test: does the admission
narrow your market? If not, it is a brag wearing a no.

---

### P6 — Category Reframe
**For:** owning the words the market uses. **Goal:** comments, followers.

```
[The thing everyone calls X.]
[Why that name causes the wrong decision.]
[What it actually is.]
[What changes once you call it that.]
```

**Fails when** it is a definition with no decision attached. A reframe has to
change what somebody does on Monday, or it is vocabulary.

---

### P7 — Process Reveal
**For:** giving away the method. **Goal:** saves, reshares, trust.

```
Here is the [n]-step [process] we run for every [engagement type].
[Each step: what happens, who does it, how long, and what it catches.]
[The step clients always want to skip, and what goes wrong when they do.]
```

**Fails when** it is a funnel diagram. The giveaway has to be genuinely useful
to someone who will never hire you. That is the price of the credibility.

---

### P8 — Document Carousel Companion
**For:** the strongest organic format on the platform. **Goal:** saves, dwell, reshares.

The caption is a different job from a post: it sets up the swipe, it does not
substitute for it. 100-200 words. See `page-surfaces.md` for the PDF specs.

```
[The question the document answers, as a statement.]
[Who it is for, specifically.]
[What is inside — the slide count and the one slide that matters most.]
[Link, in the caption, because a link inside the PDF is not tappable.]
```

**Fails when** the PDF is a repackaged slide deck with a logo on every page.
Slide 1 carries the whole hook; most readers see only it.

---

### P9 — Hiring With Friction
**For:** filtering, not attracting. **Goal:** talent.

```
We are hiring [exact role], [location/remote], [salary band].
[The honest description of the first month, including the dull part.]
[Who does well here.]
[Who would hate it.]
[How to apply, in one step.]
```

**Fails when** it says "dynamic team", "fast-paced environment", or
"competitive salary". Publishing the band is most of the post's credibility.

---

### P10 — Named Milestone
**For:** announcing without the word "thrilled". **Goal:** followers, trust.

```
[The milestone, as a specific number or named fact, with its date.]
[What it took — the unglamorous part.]
[Who did it, named.]
[What it changes for a customer. If nothing, say nothing.]
```

**Fails when** the milestone matters only internally. An office move, a
certification nobody asks for, and a work anniversary are not posts unless
something in them is useful to a reader.

---

### P11 — Customer Question Answered
**For:** inexhaustible, cheap, and consistently the best-performing Page content.
**Goal:** comments, inbound.

```
A [buyer role] asked us this week: "[the question, close to verbatim]"
[The answer, in full, including the part that does not favour you.]
[What the question tells you about where the market is.]
```

**Fails when** the question is invented or sanded down into a FAQ. Keep the
awkward phrasing; it is the proof it was real. Never quote a named person or an
identifiable account without clearance (`claims-and-compliance.md`).

---

### P12 — Post-mortem
**For:** the highest-trust format that exists, and the one most Pages refuse.
**Goal:** trust, comments, reshares.

```
[What broke, when, and for how long.]
[Who it affected.]
[The actual cause, in technical detail.]
[What we changed so it cannot recur.]
[What we still do not know.]
```

**Fails when** it is written to deflect. A post-mortem that blames a vendor,
the weather or "an unexpected combination of factors" costs more trust than
silence would have. Needs sign-off under `claims-and-compliance.md`, because an
incident disclosure can carry contractual consequences.

---

### P13 — Comparison Table
**For:** buyer education, and the format that gets saved most. **Goal:** saves, comments.

```
[Two or three real approaches to the same job.]
[Three or four dimensions that actually decide between them — cost, time to
value, who has to maintain it, what breaks at scale.]
[When each one wins, stated plainly, including when yours loses.]
```

**Fails when** your option wins every row. A comparison where you win
everything is an ad and readers price it as one. Name the case where you are
the wrong choice; it is the only line that makes the other rows believable.

---

### P14 — Event Pre-brief
**For:** filling a seat, not announcing a date. **Goal:** registrations.

```
[What will actually happen in the room, in specific terms.]
[Who should come — and who should not.]
[The one thing an attendee leaves with.]
[Date, time with timezone, duration, format, link.]
```

**Fails when** it leads with the speaker's job title instead of what the
attendee gets.

---

### P15 — Poll With A Thesis
**For:** a poll that teaches. **Goal:** comments, cheap audience research.

The question is 140 characters. The value is the comment you leave on your own
poll explaining why you are asking and what you expect — and the follow-up post
reporting the result, which is where the actual content is.

```
[Question with 3-4 options that each represent a real, defensible position.]
[First comment: why we are asking, what we predict, what we will do with it.]
```

**Fails when** the options are obviously ranked, or the poll never gets its
follow-up post. An unresolved poll is engagement bait.

---

### P16 — Employee Spotlight, With Substance
**For:** employer brand that is not a stock photo. **Goal:** talent, employee reshares.

```
[Named person, actual role.]
[One specific thing they fixed or built, with the detail.]
[Something true and particular about how they work.]
[What they are working on next.]
```

**Fails when** it is a birthday, a "fun fact", or "meet the team". The person's
work has to be the subject, or the post flatters nobody and the employee will
not reshare it.

## Format mix over a month

For a Page posting 2-4 times a week, roughly:

- **One P4 or P7 per month.** The heavy asset. It becomes the article
  (`article-architectures.md`) and the document carousel, and it is what
  employees reshare. If only one thing gets made this month, make this.
- **Two P1 or P12 per month.** Proof and honesty. These do the believing.
- **Weekly P11.** Cheapest high-quality content available and it never runs out.
- **One P5, P6 or P13 per month.** The position-taking post, for comments.
- **P9 and P16 as hiring demands.** Never on a schedule.
- **P10 and P14 when an actual event exists.** Never manufactured.

A month with no P4, P7 or P12 in it is a month of filler, regardless of volume.

## Anti-formats

Refuse these. They are the genres that make Pages unreadable, and producing one
costs more follower trust than skipping a week would.

- "We are thrilled/excited/proud to announce" anything.
- Holiday and awareness-day posts with no connection to the business.
- Inspirational quote over a stock image.
- "Happy Monday / Friday feeling" with no content.
- Rebroadcasting an industry news story with no position taken on it.
- Award announcements without the criteria.
- Office-life photo dumps with no named person and nothing happening.
- "Comment X and we'll send you Y" (named by LinkedIn's March 2026
  authenticity update).
- A question as the first line of the post.
- Any post whose entire content is a link to the blog.
- Anniversary and follower-milestone posts ("we just hit 1,000 followers!").
