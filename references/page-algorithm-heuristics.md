# Page reach economics, 2026

Read this before planning anything. Every other decision in this bundle follows
from one structural fact, and a plan built without it will look reasonable and
fail quietly for months.

## The fact

**A company Page is not a reach channel. It is an authority and conversion
asset whose distribution has to be borrowed from people.**

The 2026 numbers, from several independent directions:

| Measure | Company Page | Personal profile | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median engagement rate | 1-2% | ~4.7% | Sprout Social Q1 2026 Index, 52M+ posts |
| Share of feed distribution | ~5% | ~65% | SocialPilot Sept 2026 algorithm analysis |
| Organic reach vs follower count | ~1.18-1.2% | 8-15% | SMM News 2026 report, 524 B2B brand pages |
| Reach change 2024 → early 2026 | down 60-66% | down ~50% | Ordinal / AuthoredUp / Brenton Way, via multiple roundups |
| Median reactions per post (B2B) | 23 | 39 (founder/exec) | Imagine AI State of B2B LinkedIn, Oct 2026 |
| Reach per post vs employee posts | baseline | +561% | SocialPilot Sept 2026 |

Practitioner estimates of the gap range from 5x to 20x depending on who is
counting and what they are counting. The range matters less than the direction,
which every source agrees on. Treat the specific multiplier as contested and
the conclusion as settled.

**The honest reading:** a Page with 20,000 followers can reach fewer people than
a founder with 2,000. If a plan's growth depends on Page post volume, the plan
does not work. If a plan uses the Page as the place a buyer lands, checks you
out, and converts, and borrows reach from people and from owned channels, it
works fine.

## What follows from that

Four jobs a Page actually does well. Anything a skill in this bundle produces
should be doing one of them.

1. **Pass the credibility check.** A buyer who met a founder, saw an ad, or got
   a cold email opens the Page before replying. It has to answer "are these
   people real, do they do this specific thing, have they done it for someone
   like me" in about fifteen seconds. This is the highest-value surface in the
   whole account and it is a static asset, not a feed.
2. **Hold the assets that get borrowed.** Document carousels, articles,
   research, case studies. Employees and the founder reshare *these*; the Page
   is the library they come from.
3. **Own a distribution channel the feed cannot throttle.** A Newsletter
   notifies subscribers by email. That is the only Page surface whose reach is
   not at the algorithm's discretion, which makes it structurally the most
   valuable thing a Page can start.
4. **Convert.** Custom button, lead-gen forms, Products and Services tabs, and
   the DM inbox. Pages measured on impressions look dead; the same Pages
   measured on button clicks and form fills often are not.

## Format effects, on the Page's own posts

These hold for the small reach a Page does get, so they are worth getting right
even though they do not change the economics above.

- **Document posts (PDF carousels) are the strongest organic format.** About
  6.6% engagement, and carousels run roughly 39% more reach than the average
  post (Zoomsphere / Contentin 2026 roundups of Socialinsider and Metricool
  data). Swipe depth produces dwell time, which is what the ranker rewards.
- **Dwell time, saves and substantive comments are what the 2026 ranker
  optimises for.** Not reactions. A post that earns 8 long comments beats one
  that earns 80 likes.
- **External links in the body are deprioritised.** Put the link in the first
  comment, or for a document post in the caption, where it is tappable.
- **Generic AI-written content is actively discounted.** 360Brew was built to
  identify low-credibility content signals, and a July 2026 "AI slop" report
  button is reported to cut flagged posts' views by around 40%. Corporate
  Pages are the most likely thing in the feed to trip this, because
  press-release voice and AI voice share most of their tells. See
  `brand-voice-rules.md`.
- **What stopped working:** engagement pods (detected at around 97% accuracy),
  hashtag stacking, early-hour posting tricks, and comment-gate CTAs ("comment
  X and I'll send Y"), which LinkedIn's March 2026 authenticity update named
  directly.
- **Cadence:** 2-4 Page posts a week with substance beats daily filler. A Page
  posting daily with nothing to say trains its own followers to scroll past it,
  and the follower-feed penalty compounds. The exception is a Page in an active
  campaign window, where tempo is carrying a launch.
- **Timing:** Tuesday to Thursday mornings in the audience's timezone remains
  the default for B2B. Treat this as a tiebreaker, not a lever. Posting time
  has never rescued a post nobody wanted to read.

## How to read every number on this page

All of it measures someone else's corpus. Most of it is vendor data with a
proprietary scoring method behind it, and vendors benchmark the formats they
sell tooling for. Specifically:

- Reach and engagement medians are **relative** guidance. Your Page's own
  baseline from `metrics-taxonomy.md` beats any benchmark here.
- A multiplier quoted without a denominator ("561% more reach") compares two
  things that were not measured the same way. Useful as a direction, useless as
  a forecast.
- Where this file and a client's own analytics disagree, the analytics win.

This file is **last** in the precedence order (`precedence.md`). It never
justifies a claim the business cannot stand behind, and it never overrides
something the Page owner has told you.
