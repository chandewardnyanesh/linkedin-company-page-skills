Why company pages need their own skills

A LinkedIn company page is not a reach channel. It is an authority and conversion asset whose distribution has to be borrowed from people. The 2026 data, from several independent directions:

So a page with 20,000 followers can reach fewer people than a founder with 2,000. Any content plan whose growth depends on page post volume does not work.

This is also why a personal-brand skill bundle cannot simply be pointed at a company page. The personal formula libraries are built on devices a brand cannot use honestly — a confession hook needs someone to confess, and a company has no self. The attempt produces the worst genre on the platform: a corporate entity performing feelings.

What a page has instead is access a person does not have: the whole book of business, the aggregate numbers, the thing that broke in production, the pattern across forty clients, the real cost of the work. A person can report their own experience. A page can report a population. Every format in this project is a way of spending that advantage.

The four jobs a company page actually does well

  • Pass the credibility check — the buyer who met you elsewhere and opened the page before replying. Small, late-funnel, the most valuable traffic the account gets.
  • Hold the assets that get borrowed — carousels, articles and research that employees reshare.
  • Own distribution the feed cannot throttle — a LinkedIn newsletter notifies subscribers by email.
  • Convert — custom button, lead-gen forms, Products and Services tabs.

Installation

No API keys, no signup, no Python packages. Python 3.10+ for the helper scripts.

git clone https://github.com/chandewardnyanesh/linkedin-company-page-skills.git
cd linkedin-company-page-skills
python -m scripts.selftest      # 19 checks, offline

Then add the cloned directory as a plugin marketplace in Claude Code — the repo ships .claude-plugin/marketplace.json. For Claude Desktop or claude.ai, upload any individual skills/<name>/SKILL.md, or the whole directory where your plan supports plugins. Each skill works standalone.

Optional: Google Search Console, Ahrefs or Semrush connectors replace inferred search demand with measured demand in trend-radar and page-performance-report. Without them, those skills label their evidence as observed and say so.

The 13 skills

Setting up

page-profile-builder

Crawls the company website and LinkedIn page, writes the canonical brand profile, proof library, ICP and competitor set every other skill reads. Run this first.

page-optimizer

Audits and rewrites the page itself — tagline (120 chars), About (2,000 chars), specialties, custom button, Products and Services tabs, Featured, Life tab.

Deciding what to say

trend-radar

Ranked content opportunities with graded evidence — measured, observed or inferred, never conflated.

competitor-page-analyzer

Competitor cadence, format mix, newsletters, employees posting — and the white space you have standing to own.

page-content-calendar

A 30-day multi-surface plan built on real capacity rather than a posting target.

Writing

page-post-writer

Page posts across 16 brand formats — proof ledger, build log, teardown, original data drop, honest no, post-mortem, comparison table — picked by the job the post has to do.

authority-article-writer

LinkedIn Articles and newsletter issues across 8 authority architectures, with newsletter eligibility checked first.

page-humanizer

Strips AI tells and press-release voice. --mode audit runs a six-gate pre-publish review including claim clearance.

Reusing what exists

page-repurposer

One source into several native assets — blog to carousel, webinar to posts, a founder's personal post to a page version.

page-asset-extractor

--mode pattern deconstructs any high-performing post. --mode proof mines documents for the claims you are entitled to make.

Distribution and inbound

employee-amplification

Per-person reshare kits with distinct angles, the once-a-week notify budget, Thought Leader Ad selection. This is the distribution layer.

page-engagement-desk

Comment replies, complaint triage and escalation, commenting as the page.

Measuring

page-performance-report

A monthly report that says what to change, measured against your own baseline rather than borrowed benchmarks.

Three design decisions

1. No figure publishes without a cleared proof row

A number on a company page is a representation the business made, in public, with a timestamp, to an audience that includes its customers and its competitors. It gets screenshotted before it gets corrected.

So every writing skill stops at a claim with no entry in the proof library. A format's [specific number] slot is not a licence to produce a plausible one — plausible is fabricated. When the library has nothing that fits, the skill asks, drops the claim, or changes format. It does not soften an unevidenced number into "up to".

2. The humanizer fixes two voices, not one

Brand copy sounds like AI and like a press release, and the two reinforce each other because they share most of their tells. Two tests on every opening line:

  • Swap in a competitor's name. Survives? It says nothing. → press-release voice
  • Swap in a different industry. Survives? Cut it. → AI voice

Both resolve the same way, and it is not word substitution — "utilise" for "leverage" is the same empty slot with a different word in it. The fix is a named person, a specific number, or a dated event.

3. Manual publishing is the honest answer, not a missing feature

SurfacePublishable by API?
Page post, document post, commentYes — w_organization_social + admin role
LinkedIn Article (Articles for Pages)No. No publishing API exists
LinkedIn NewsletterNo. An extension of articles
Poll, EventNo
Tagline, About, specialties, tabs, FeaturedNo

Any tool claiming to automate a LinkedIn article is driving a browser or is wrong. This project ships draft-first on every surface and says so plainly rather than implying a key would change it.

To automate the API-reachable surfaces, export a poster command in a trusted shell: export LINKEDIN_PAGE_CUSTOM_POSTER='/path/to/your-poster'. The variable is deliberately ignored inside .env files — a file on disk choosing which executable runs is a code-execution hole dressed as configuration.

Frequently asked questions

Is this for personal LinkedIn profiles or company pages?

Company pages specifically. The formats, voice constraints, claim liability and distribution model are all different. For personal-profile work, see sergebulaev/linkedin-skills, whose structural conventions this project follows.

Does it post to LinkedIn automatically?

Not by default, and for articles and newsletters not at all — LinkedIn has no publishing API for either. Page posts and comments can be automated by wiring up your own poster command. Everything passes through a human approval card first.

Do I need an API key, a scraper or a paid tool?

No. There are no dependencies, the library is Python standard library only, and the default path makes no network call.

Will this make AI-written posts undetectable?

No, and the project does not claim it. No edit reliably beats a trained classifier, and detector scores shift between model generations. What the rules do is make copy specific, attributable and checkable, which is what a human reader responds to.

Can it write a LinkedIn newsletter for a company page?

Yes, and it checks eligibility first: Super Admin permission, at least 150 followers, an active page with recent posts, and a history of original content. Publishing is manual because no API exists. A newsletter matters because subscribers are notified by email, making it the only page surface whose delivery does not depend on the feed algorithm.

How is this different from asking Claude to "write a LinkedIn post"?

Three things. It refuses to invent statistics about your business. It picks a format based on the job the post has to do rather than defaulting to a hook. And it plans the employee amplification without which a page post reaches almost nobody.

Does it work for non-English pages?

The voice rules and formats are language-agnostic in structure, and the company profile records the publishing language. The banned-vocabulary lists are English; contributions for other languages are welcome.

Is it safe to point at my company website?

Yes. Everything fetched is treated as data, never as instructions, and a figure found on your own website is not treated as evidence until a human clears it — which is how stale claims get republished with fresh timestamps.

What this project does not claim

  • It does not beat AI detectors. No edit reliably does, and claiming otherwise would be dishonest.
  • It does not promise reach. Every benchmark above measures someone else's corpus, most of it vendor data with a proprietary score behind it. Your own trailing baseline beats all of them.
  • It does not attribute revenue to posts. A company page cannot show which post produced a deal, and the reporting skill says so in every report rather than modelling what the data will not support.
  • It does not automate LinkedIn articles or newsletters. Nothing can.
